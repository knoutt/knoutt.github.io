Desktop Flight Radar

A palm-sized instrument that lives under my monitor and shows every aircraft over Toronto in real time.

The round touchscreen draws a city basemap with live traffic, each plane tagged with its callsign, type and altitude. Tap one and it pulls up a photo of that exact airframe, the airline, route, altitude, ground speed, distance, track and climb rate.

PC Volume Mixer

Physical faders for per-app volume, so Discord, Spotify and a game never have to fight over one slider.

Four faders each control a program, with the strip display mirroring every level and app icon. Three keys page between fader banks, and a companion desktop app maps any program to any fader and talks to the mixer over USB. The fader caps are printed dice: count the pips for the channel number.

Toronto, CA

Most of what I build starts as a small annoyance at my own desk and ends as a finished object.

I'm a mechanical engineering student drawn to the automotive, aerospace and tech industries. I like projects that cross disciplines, where the enclosure, the electronics and the code all have to agree with each other before anything works.

Outside of coursework that means reverse-engineering parts that bother me, designing hardware that fits how I actually work, and seeing it through to something I use every day.
