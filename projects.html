App Volume Mixer

Enclosure, PCB, firmware and a Windows app

Windows lets you set a separate volume for every app, but only by digging through a menu. This puts it on four physical faders. Each fader is assigned to one program, and the screen above shows every channel's level in that program's colour. The LED by each fader matches.

BANK− and BANK+ page through more apps when four isn't enough. A companion app on the PC decides which program goes on which fader and talks to the mixer over USB-C.

The fader caps are dice, so the pips tell you which channel is which.

The board, rev A

  • Four slide potentiometers, one per channel
  • Three mechanical switches: BANK−, SEL, BANK+
  • Header for the SPI display
  • UART header
  • USB-C to the PC

Desktop Flight Radar

Round touchscreen, live aircraft data, printed enclosure

A small round screen that shows the planes over Toronto right now. One view is a city map with every aircraft labelled by callsign, type and altitude. The other is a radar scope with range rings.

Tap a plane and it pulls up a photo of that exact aircraft from planespotters.net, the airline, where it's flying from and to, its altitude, speed, heading, climb rate and how far it is from my desk.

The bezel screws on, so it can be printed in any colour.

Three-screen photo display

ESP32, SPI displays, ESP-NOW

Three tiny screens in a scale model, flipping through photos one after another. A Valentine's Day gift for my girlfriend.

Each screen has its own ESP32, SPI TFT and microSD card. One unit runs the slideshow and broadcasts the timing over ESP-NOW so the other two stay in step.

Most of the work was in getting it reliable: a custom image pipeline for the microcontrollers, two devices sharing one SPI bus, display orientation, colour correction, and SD cards that kept dropping out.

FieldMesh

LoRa mesh, Raspberry Pi, FastAPI

A mesh communications and sensing kit that works with no internet at all. The nodes are LilyGO T-Beams (ESP32, LoRa and GPS) running Meshtastic, and each one is both a data source and a relay.

A Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W is plugged into one T-Beam over USB and hosts a Wi-Fi dashboard that opens in any browser. One FastAPI service connects the radio to that dashboard: it cleans up incoming packets, keeps live node state in memory with optional SQLite history, and streams updates over server-sent events behind a simple local login.

PC case and GPU covers

Reverse engineering, ABS printing

The Lian-Li O11 Vision Compact has a large empty space above the motherboard. I measured and modelled the stock grommet cover, then designed a sculpted replacement that fills the gap, lines up with the case's own geometry and hides the cables.

The RTX 5090 came with a loud, angular paint job and no lighting. I modelled a tight-fitting ABS shroud that covers the stock styling and has a channel for an LED strip.
